# Messaging Guide

## Status and Scope

This guide defines messaging direction for later design and copywriting. It is not final website copy, legal copy, advertising approval, or regulatory verification.

All sample headlines, taglines, calls to action, and phrasing are drafts unless already approved in `docs/MASTER_BUILD_SPEC.md` or `docs/DECISIONS.md`.

## Brand Positioning

**DOT Career Protection Program** is a practical membership program for DOT-regulated workers who want preventive drug-and-alcohol education and defined support if an eligible future DOT violation occurs.

The brand should feel:

- Protective, not punitive.
- Practical, not academic.
- Clear, not legalistic.
- Respectful, not judgmental.
- Transparent, not evasive.
- Independent and professional, not government-branded.

The domain `49CFR40.com` should support the brand but not replace the program name.

## Core Value Proposition

Membership helps workers prepare before a crisis by providing ongoing preventive education now and defined benefit categories if an eligible future DOT drug or alcohol violation occurs after membership requirements are met.

## Primary Message

Draft primary message:

> Protect the career you worked to build with practical DOT drug-and-alcohol education and defined eligible support if a future violation happens later.

This wording is directional and should be refined during final copywriting.

## Supporting Messages

- Membership must begin before the violation or refusal to apply to that event.
- RTD-related benefits require 90 consecutive days of active membership.
- Preventive education and ordinary member communications may begin immediately.
- Educational activities are optional and are not conditions for receiving benefits.
- Benefits are subject to eligibility requirements, program terms, and approved arrangements.
- The program does not guarantee a SAP outcome, return to duty, employment, licensing, reinstatement, or compliance.
- Member support is for general program questions, educational resources, and next-step navigation during regular business hours.

## Audience-Specific Messaging

### CDL drivers and FMCSA-covered workers

Emphasize:

- Protecting a driving career.
- Practical risk awareness around DOT testing, refusals, timing, alcohol, cannabis, medications, stimulants, and testing decisions.
- The difference between preparing before a violation and seeking help after one.
- FMCSA-specific claims require verification before publication.

Avoid:

- Treating all drivers as careless.
- Implying all violations are intentional.
- Implying all violations are accidental.
- Extending FMCSA details to every DOT agency.

### Other DOT safety-sensitive workers

Emphasize:

- DOT-regulated safety-sensitive work beyond trucking.
- Broad transportation roles such as aviation, public transportation, rail, pipeline, and maritime.
- Similar membership structure, prices, and general benefit categories, subject to terms.
- Agency-specific details require official verification.

Avoid:

- Calling these workers a later launch audience.
- Using trucking-only examples.
- Presenting FMCSA-specific rules as universal.

### Employers and teams

Emphasize:

- Request-information path for education and training inquiries.
- Workforce education, safety-oriented sessions, and general employer resources as possible services requiring later business approval.
- Employer services should support, not dominate, the worker membership message.

Avoid:

- Promising training that has not been developed.
- Offering legal advice, compliance guarantees, or employer representation.
- Suggesting employer training replaces required official or regulatory training unless verified and approved.

### Existing members

Emphasize:

- General program questions.
- Educational resources.
- Next-step navigation.
- Staff follow-up through appropriate channels.

Avoid collecting or requesting:

- Clinical records.
- Treatment records.
- Laboratory reports.
- SAP reports.
- Employer documents.
- Detailed drug-use histories.
- Medical information.
- Uploaded violation documents.

## FMCSA-Driver Messaging

Draft direction:

- Lead with career protection and practical preparation.
- Explain that DOT violations can happen through more than intentional drug use or obvious impairment.
- Present preventive education as a way to understand common risk patterns and decisions.
- Make the join-before-violation rule and 90-day waiting period prominent.
- Use FMCSA language only where verified against current official sources before publication.

Suggested draft headings:

- `Protect the career you worked to build.`
- `DOT risk is easier to plan for before there is a problem.`
- `Education now. Defined eligible support later.`
- `Join before a violation, not after.`

## Messaging for Other DOT Safety-Sensitive Workers

Draft direction:

- Lead with DOT safety-sensitive work broadly.
- Use examples across transportation sectors without overexplaining rules.
- State that eligibility and processes may vary by applicable DOT agency and program terms.
- Link to benefits, eligibility, FAQ, and join pages.

Suggested draft headings:

- `Protection-minded education for DOT safety-sensitive workers.`
- `Not a truck driver? The same planning principle still matters.`
- `Know the limits before you join.`

## Employer and Team-Training Messaging

Draft direction:

- Position employer services as education-focused and request-based.
- Use careful language such as `may include` until services are approved.
- Avoid letting employer messaging overshadow worker membership.

Suggested draft headings:

- `DOT drug-and-alcohol education for teams.`
- `Request information about workforce education.`
- `Training details require business review before publication.`

## Approved Tone and Voice

Use language that is:

- Calm.
- Practical.
- Direct.
- Respectful.
- Plain-spoken.
- Worker-facing.
- Transparent about limits.
- Easy to scan on mobile.

Avoid language that is:

- Shaming.
- Panic-driven.
- Overly legalistic.
- Overly clinical.
- Accusatory.
- Moralizing.
- Vague about limitations.
- Written like a government notice.

## Language and Claims That Require Caution

Use caution with any claim about:

- DOT, FMCSA, or agency-specific rules.
- 49 CFR Part 40 requirements.
- SAP process steps.
- Refusals and violation consequences.
- Return-to-duty and follow-up testing.
- Employer obligations.
- Eligibility calculations.
- Waiting periods and benefit periods.
- Cancellation, refunds, automatic renewal, and re-enrollment.
- Provider participation.
- Course content or availability.
- Legal, medical, treatment, clinical, or compliance outcomes.

## Prohibited, Unsupported, or Misleading Claims

Do not claim or imply:

- Guaranteed return to duty.
- Guaranteed continued employment.
- Guaranteed job placement.
- Guaranteed employer referral or employer network access.
- Guaranteed licensing or reinstatement.
- Guaranteed favorable SAP decision.
- Guaranteed compliance.
- That membership prevents every violation.
- That the program provides legal advice.
- That the program provides medical advice.
- That the program represents the worker to an employer.
- That any SAP chosen by the member will be reimbursed.
- That treatment costs are automatically included.
- That follow-up, random, pre-employment, post-accident, reasonable-suspicion, separate-violation, or unlimited retesting is included.
- That optional education is required for benefit eligibility.
- That public forms should collect clinical, laboratory, treatment, SAP-report, medical, or detailed drug-use information.

## Disclosure-Writing Principles

Disclosures should be:

- Plain-language.
- Close to the related claim or CTA.
- Visible on mobile.
- Repeated where the user is likely to make a decision.
- Not hidden only in a footer or legal page.
- Specific about what is subject to eligibility, waiting periods, approved arrangements, and final terms.

Disclosures should not be used to rescue a misleading headline. The headline and body copy should already be accurate.

## Examples of Wording to Avoid

Avoid:

- `Guaranteed return to work.`
- `We will get your CDL back.`
- `Never worry about DOT testing again.`
- `Unlimited SAP help.`
- `All treatment costs covered.`
- `Use any SAP and we pay.`
- `Join after a violation and we will handle it.`
- `Compliance guaranteed.`
- `Required monthly quizzes keep your benefits active.`
- `DOT-approved protection plan` unless official approval is verified and legally cleared.
- `Legal help for your violation` unless a legal service is actually approved and provided.

## Interior-Page Messaging Hierarchy

### How It Works

Lead with the timeline: join before a violation, receive education, maintain active membership, complete waiting period, contact the program if an eligible event occurs, eligibility review, manual coordination.

### Membership Benefits

Lead with included categories, then separate conditions and exclusions.

### Eligibility and Limitations

Lead with the most important limits, not with marketing copy.

### Education and Resources

Lead with helpful public education and careful regulatory-verification notes.

### Employer and Team Training

Lead with request-information framing and business-review caveats.

### Legal and policy pages

Lead with structural purpose and final-review status. Do not invent substantive terms.

## Distinction Between Education, Support, Benefits, and Guarantees

### Education

Education means preventive emails, public resources, self-checks, quizzes, risk-awareness prompts, and practical information. Education helps members understand and think through risk; it does not guarantee behavior, compliance, or outcomes.

### Support

Support means general program questions, educational resources, and next-step navigation during regular business hours. Support does not mean legal advice, medical advice, employer representation, SAP decision-making, interpretation of lab results, immediate response, or unlimited consulting.

### Benefits

Benefits are defined membership categories that may be available after eligibility requirements and program terms are met. Benefits include the approved provisional categories, but exact details require final terms and operational review.

### Guarantees

The program must not guarantee return to duty, employment, licensing, reinstatement, a SAP outcome, compliance, job placement, or prevention of every violation.
