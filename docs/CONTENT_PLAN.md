# Content Plan

## Status and Scope

This document develops the documentation-only content strategy for the DOT Career Protection Program public website. It is not final website copy, a legal document, a regulatory verification record, or an implementation plan.

This content plan is controlled by:

1. `docs/MASTER_BUILD_SPEC.md`
2. `docs/DECISIONS.md`
3. `docs/OPEN_ITEMS.md`

The current-site audit remains unauthorized and has not been performed. Any future use of the existing website must wait until the audit is expressly authorized.

## Website Purpose

The public website should explain and sell membership in the **DOT Career Protection Program** for workers who want preventive DOT drug-and-alcohol education and defined support if an eligible future DOT drug or alcohol violation occurs.

The website should help visitors understand:

- Why membership must begin before a violation or refusal.
- What preventive education is intended to do.
- What benefit categories may be available after eligibility requirements are met.
- What the membership does not promise.
- How to join monthly or annually.
- Where to review eligibility, limitations, terms, and disclosures before joining.

The site should be credible and useful without becoming a regulation encyclopedia.

## Content Goals

### Primary goals

- Drive qualified visitors to join monthly or annually.
- Make the join-before-violation rule clear before checkout.
- Make the 90-day waiting period clear before checkout.
- Explain benefits in plain language while keeping eligibility and terms visible.
- Help workers understand that preventive education is immediate but RTD-related benefits are conditional.
- Build trust through clarity, transparency, and respectful worker-facing language.

### Secondary goals

- Provide enough public education to answer common questions and support search visibility.
- Direct non-FMCSA DOT workers to an audience-appropriate landing page.
- Give employers a separate path for training inquiries without letting employer services dominate the worker membership message.
- Provide clear routes to contact, member help, legal/policy pages, and resource content.
- Prepare content structures for later design, copywriting, legal review, regulatory verification, and implementation.

## Primary Audience

The primary website audience is:

- CDL drivers.
- Commercial motor vehicle drivers.
- Other FMCSA-covered safety-sensitive workers.

Content for this audience should be practical, direct, and easy to read quickly on a phone. It should acknowledge real-world risk without shame or panic.

## Secondary Audiences

### Other DOT safety-sensitive workers

A separate landing page at `/dot-workers` should speak to workers covered by other DOT agencies, such as aviation, public transportation, rail, pipeline, maritime, and other DOT-regulated safety-sensitive roles. This audience should not be treated as an afterthought.

### Employers and teams

Employers, safety managers, compliance staff, and team leaders may seek workforce education or training information. Employer content should remain separate from the primary worker membership path.

### Existing members

Existing members may need help understanding general program questions, educational resources, and next-step navigation. Public member-help content should avoid collecting clinical, medical, laboratory, treatment, SAP-report, employer-document, or detailed drug-use information.

### Legal, compliance, and business reviewers

These reviewers need clear markers showing which claims require regulatory verification, owner review, business review, operational review, or legal review before publication.

## Audience Needs, Questions, Concerns, and Misconceptions

### Worker needs

- Understand whether the program is for them.
- Understand the cost and billing options.
- Know what is included, what is not included, and what is subject to eligibility.
- Understand when benefits become available.
- Understand whether joining after a violation helps with that violation.
- Know how the program differs from legal advice, medical advice, SAP decision-making, or employer representation.
- Feel respected rather than judged.

### Common worker questions

- Can I join after a DOT drug or alcohol violation?
- What happens if the violation happened before I joined?
- Does the 90-day waiting period apply if I pay annually?
- What benefits may be available after the waiting period?
- Who chooses or approves the SAP provider?
- Does this pay for treatment?
- Does this guarantee I get back to work?
- Are educational emails required to keep benefits?
- What if I am not an FMCSA driver but work in another DOT-regulated role?

### Concerns to address

- Fear of being judged.
- Confusion about 49 CFR Part 40 and agency-specific rules.
- Worry about hidden exclusions or waiting periods.
- Skepticism about whether the program overpromises.
- Uncertainty about the difference between education, support, benefits, and guarantees.
- Privacy concerns about forms and sensitive information.

### Misconceptions to correct carefully

- Misconception: A worker can wait to join until after a violation and use benefits for that violation.
- Misconception: Paying annually removes the waiting period.
- Misconception: A prior violation means another violation could never happen.
- Misconception: Every violation results from deliberate drug use or obvious impairment.
- Misconception: Membership guarantees a favorable SAP decision or return to work.
- Misconception: Optional education is required to qualify for benefits.
- Misconception: The same details always apply across all DOT agencies.

## Public Website Content Versus Member-Email Content

### Public website content

Public content should:

- Explain the membership structure at a high level.
- Present benefit categories and limitations.
- Provide basic education and resource-library articles.
- Lead visitors toward joining or reviewing eligibility.
- Avoid collecting sensitive clinical or document-heavy information.
- Mark regulatory claims for official-source verification before publication.

### Member-email content

Member emails may include:

- Ongoing preventive education.
- Monthly educational reminders.
- Self-check questionnaires.
- Brief quizzes.
- Safety-reflection exercises.
- Risk-awareness checkups.
- Onboarding and welcome messages.
- Member-help links.

Member-email engagement must not be described as a condition of benefit eligibility. Email content and workflows remain operationally unresolved until HubSpot setup and content cadence are approved.

- Use plain-language educational framing.
- Separate FMCSA-specific material from broader DOT material.
- Include verification notes for regulatory claims before publication.
- Add clear CTAs from articles to membership, benefits, eligibility, and contact pages.

## Trust and Credibility Strategy

Trust should come from specificity, restraint, and transparency.

### Build trust by doing

- State the price clearly.
- State the join-before-violation rule clearly.
- State the 90-day waiting period clearly.
- Separate included benefits from exclusions and conditions.
- Identify where final terms control.
- Avoid inflated claims.
- Avoid legal, medical, SAP, employment, or compliance guarantees.
- Explain how regulatory content will be verified.
- Provide easy access to policy and legal pages once finalized.

### Avoid false credibility signals

- Do not use government seals, fake badges, or imagery implying official government endorsement.
- Do not imply the program is a DOT agency.
- Do not imply guaranteed employer acceptance.
- Do not publish unverified regulatory summaries as final.

## Regulatory-Verification Process

Before public publication of regulatory statements:

1. Identify whether the statement is general DOT, FMCSA-specific, or another DOT-agency-specific claim.
2. Verify against current official DOT or applicable DOT-agency sources.
3. Record the official source URL.
4. Record the date verified.
5. Confirm the plain-language summary does not change the legal meaning.
6. Flag unresolved or conflicting agency-specific details.
7. Route legal/regulatory claims for review before publication when appropriate.

Until verification is complete, content should use labels such as:

- `Regulatory verification required before publication.`
- `FMCSA-specific statement; verify against current FMCSA/DOT sources before publication.`
- `General DOT statement; confirm agency-specific differences before publication.`

## FMCSA-Specific Versus Broader DOT Content Boundaries

### FMCSA-specific content

The homepage and CDL-focused resources may use FMCSA and commercial-driving examples. These pages should not imply that FMCSA details apply identically to aviation, transit, rail, pipeline, maritime, or other DOT contexts.

### Broader DOT content

The `/dot-workers` page should use broader DOT-worker language and examples. It should avoid trucking-exclusive examples and avoid agency-specific detail unless verified and clearly labeled.

### Boundary rule

When a claim could differ by DOT agency, write it as a general program explanation and flag the regulatory detail for official-source verification instead of overgeneralizing.
